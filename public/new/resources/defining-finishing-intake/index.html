When does RAS make sense for mud crab fattening?

A planning guide for hard-shell fattening and finishing, from buyer grade and crab supply to the cost of running a recirculating system.

Start with the job the farm needs to do

If the job is to take in market-size hard-shell crabs and improve their condition for a buyer, start by defining what that buyer will accept. Fattening and finishing are different jobs from raising juveniles in a nursery or growing smaller stock in a pond. A soft-shell operation is different again: it has to detect moults and harvest at the right time.

A recirculating aquaculture system (RAS) treats and reuses water. Individual compartments can also give operators a consistent place to observe, feed and handle each crab. That makes RAS worth assessing when separate handling and tighter control of the receiving environment matter. If crabs arrive irregularly, power backup is unresolved, or there is no buyer specification, work through those questions before committing to equipment.

Work backwards from what the buyer accepts

  1. Buyer specificationGrade, condition, quantity and delivery dates
  2. Intake requirementsSpecies, size, condition and expected weekly supply
  3. Stock and handlingWho supplies, grades, transports and receives the crabs

Ask where the stock comes from, how much can arrive in a typical week, and which weeks are likely to fall short. Ask the buyer how they grade and reject crabs, not just how many kilograms they want.

Agree who checks the crabs on arrival and what gets recorded. For a trial, track what was received, what reached the target grade and what the buyer actually accepted. Those are different quantities.

What drives RAS setup and running costs?

Build costs

Compartments and racks, plumbing, water treatment, pumps and aeration, backup power, and site work such as drainage and electrical connections.

Running costs

Stock, feed, energy, water and routine testing, daily labour, cleaning and maintenance. Allow for batches that fall short of the buyer’s grade.

Two facilities with the same box count can have very different costs. Incoming size and condition affect how long a compartment is occupied. Water treatment, backup power and staffing depend on the site and how the farm runs. Compare options against the buyer-accepted output you need, not just the purchase price per box.

What to bring before asking for a quote

  • A site sketch, available floor area, water source and power or backup arrangements.
  • The stock species, size and condition, likely supplier and realistic weekly availability.
  • The buyer’s grade, handling requirements, weekly quantity and delivery timing.
  • The decision you need to make: rule out the site, compare layouts, or scope a first trial.
